Caravan and Road Trip

Australia is big, and most of it is reached by road. In 2025 Australians took 17.3 million overnight caravan and camping trips, the biggest year on record. This page follows those trips: where they go, when, how long, and why the open road still wins.

Where do the caravans go?

Overnight trips by state

Each state is shaded by how many overnight trips Australians took there in the year to June 2026. Caravan and camping trips are part of these totals, but the official figures do not split them out by state. A darker shade means more trips. Hover over a state for trips, nights and spending.

New South Wales received 39.2 million overnight trips, ahead of Victoria (31.0 million) and Queensland (26.0 million). The Northern Territory had under 1 million. A trip that visits two states counts in both.

When trips happen

Each square is one state in one three-month period, from September 2025 to June 2026. Darker squares mean more trips.

Victoria peaks in the March quarter (8.5 million trips), the end of summer, while Queensland is busiest in spring and early summer. The Northern Territory has the opposite pattern: its quietest quarter is the humid summer, with 168,000 trips.

Trips are growing

Trips rose 14% in 2025 and kept edging up to 17.7 million in the year to March 2026. Take care comparing years: the survey method changed in 2025.

Nights away

Bigger circles mean more nights spent away in the year to June 2026. Trips and nights tell different stories because some states see longer stays.

Victoria had more trips than Queensland, but Queensland had more nights (92.0 million against 86.5 million). Queensland visitors stay longer.

Who goes, and why?

A holiday on wheels

Of every 100 caravan and camping trips in the year to March 2026, 80 were holidays and 12 were visits to friends and relatives.

The average trip is now 3.4 nights: shorter and closer to home.

Where the money goes

Commercial caravan parks host 67% of trips, 70% of nights and 75% of spending. Longer stays and paid sites mean more dollars per trip.

Big states, small states

Every state, same size

On a normal map, huge Western Australia swamps the tiny ACT. Here each state gets one equal tile, placed roughly where it sits on the map, with the average length of a trip printed on it.

The Northern Territory has the longest trips at 4.3 nights. Victoria and the ACT have the shortest at 2.8 nights, which suits quick getaways.

Where to park and which way to drive

Caravan parks and campsites

Each dot is a caravan park (teal) or campsite (orange) from OpenStreetMap: 9,782 sites in total, of which 7,767 are campsites. Sites bunch along the east coast and southern coast and thin out across the outback, where the next stop can be hundreds of kilometres away.

OpenStreetMap is mapped by volunteers, so it will miss some sites and include some free bush camps.

Putting it together

Who leads on spending

Each line follows one state. Lines rise or fall as the state gains or loses rank for visitor spending. Crossing lines mean two states swapped places.

Queensland led in the September quarter, then New South Wales took over from December. Victoria climbed to second in the March quarter, its summer peak.

States compared

Each line is a state. Follow it across four measures. Every measure is scaled so the highest state scores 100, which lets different units sit on one axis.

New South Wales leads on trips, nights and spending. The Northern Territory is small on all three but tops the list for nights per trip.
